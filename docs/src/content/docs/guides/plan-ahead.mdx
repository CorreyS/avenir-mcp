**Goal:** know whether the money lasts, and when it would run out, before it does.

**Tools:** [`forecast_balance`](/avenir-mcp/reference/tools/forecast-balance/), then
[`set_category_budget`](/avenir-mcp/reference/tools/set-category-budget/).
**Prompt:** `plan_next_month`.

## What the projection assumes

| Assumption | Where it comes from | Change it with |
|---|---|---|
| Starting balance | today's balance of the open on-budget accounts | `account_ids` |
| Recurring charges and income | the same payee in 3 of the last 4 full months, each month within 20 % of its median | — (returned in `assumptions.recurring`) |
| Other spending | average of the last 3 full months, recurring charges excluded | `variable_monthly` |
| Income | what you give; otherwise the last 3 months' average | `monthly_income` |
| One-off amounts | only what you give | `one_offs` |

Details: [Forecasting](/avenir-mcp/concepts/forecasting/).

## Reading the answer

- `months[].start`, `inflows`, `outflows`, `end`: the month's balance and flows.
- `months[].lowest`: the lowest point within the month, day by day — the one that tells
  you whether a payment would bounce.
- `first_shortfall`: the first month whose lowest point is below zero, or `null`.
- `months[].end_low`, `end_high`: a range around each month-end, one standard deviation
  of how far the other spending (and the income, unless you give it) of the last 3 full
  months strayed from its average, widening month after month. In the demo plan, whose other
  spending did not vary, they equal `end`. `band.note` says how the range was made, or why
  there is none (fewer than 3 full months of history).
- `band.first_possible_shortfall`: the first month the balance could go below zero within
  that range, or `null`.

<Aside type="caution" title="A projection, not a prediction">
It extends your recent months. A holiday, a yearly tax or a one-off purchase in them
pushes the averages; a new expense is invisible until you add it. Read the assumptions
before the conclusion.
</Aside>

## The charges that come back every month

Ask *"what am I subscribed to, and what does it cost a year?"*: `find_recurring_charges`
lists what the plan pays at about the same amount most months (3 of the last 4), costliest
over a year first, with its category and whether a YNAB schedule already covers it. Regular
spending at one merchant counts too (groceries, here); a charge paid once a year does not,
unless it has a schedule.

## Are the debts going down?

Ask *"am I paying off my debts?"* or *"how did my net worth change over the year?"*:
`get_net_worth_trend` gives, at each month end, what the accounts hold (`assets`), what is
owed (`debts`, negative, as YNAB shows it) and the two together (`net_worth`). Tracking
accounts count too: here the joint savings, the car loan, a student loan paid off in March
2026 and the former bank closed in May 2026, each counted for the months it still had a
balance. Past month ends are rebuilt
from today's balances, less the transactions dated after them; the current month shows
today's.

The lender charges the car loan's interest on the loan account each month, so the debt
falls by less than the payment; a car repair and Christmas each flatten a month.

<Code code={networth} lang="json" title="get_net_worth_trend" />

## When will the debts be paid off?

Ask *"when will my car loan be paid off if I put 500 a month on it?"* or *"avalanche or
snowball?"*: `get_debt_payoff_plan` pays the debts month by month with the sum you give.
Each month every debt is charged a twelfth of its yearly rate, then gets its minimum
payment; the rest goes to the dearest debt first (avalanche) or to the smallest
(snowball), and a debt paid off frees its minimum for the next. Both orders are given by
default, with the interest the avalanche saves.

The rates and minimum payments come from the loan details kept in YNAB. A credit card has
none there: it is counted at 0 % and a note says so; give its rate in `overrides`. Without
`monthly_budget`, the minimum payments are used. Here the car loan is the only debt left,
so both orders are the same: 100 a month above its 400 payment.

<Code code={debtPayoff} lang="json" title="get_debt_payoff_plan" />

## How long would the money last?

Ask *"if I lost my income, how long could I last?"*: `get_runway` divides the money
available today by the average monthly spending of the last complete months. The money
available is the balance of the budget's checking, savings and cash accounts, less what
its credit cards owe; tracking accounts, here the joint savings and the car loan, are not
counted and are listed in `left_out`. No income is assumed. Money moved to a tracking
account that holds an asset (savings, investments) is not spending, as in
`get_savings_rate`; a loan payment is.

Name the category groups you could not stop paying in `essential_groups` for a second
runway on those alone: here the bills and everyday spending. The demo budget holds
transactions since June only, so the average is over three months, and a note says so.

<Code code={runway} lang="json" title="get_runway" />

## Which categories will run out this month?

Ask *"which categories will I overspend this month?"* or *"at this pace, how much will
Groceries need?"*: `get_category_pace` projects each category's spending to the end of the
month in progress. It does not spread the month evenly: each category follows its own
rhythm, learnt from the last complete months, the share of a month's spending it usually
has done by today. Rent paid on the 3rd is all spent by the 25th; groceries bought every
week are not. What is spent so far, divided by that share, gives the month's spending
(`history_share`). Early in the month, when that share is small, what the rest of the
month usually holds is added instead (`history_rest`); a category with less than three
months of history is scaled by the days elapsed (`linear`). What YNAB has scheduled for the
rest of the month is a floor (`scheduled`).

The categories likely to end the month below zero come first, with what they would be
short of. Here, on 25 September, Restaurants is already 22.50 over, and its history says
nothing more usually comes after the 24th; the bills are paid. Name `categories` or
`groups` to ask about some of them only.

<Code code={pace} lang="json" title="get_category_pace (excerpt)" />

## When will a category reach its goal?

Ask *"when will Holidays reach 2,000?"* or *"how much a month to get there by March?"*:
`get_goal_projection` takes the pace at which the category's balance grew over the last
complete months, what was assigned to it less what was spent out of it, and adds it each
month to what the category holds today. It gives the month the goal is reached, or `null`
when the pace never gets there; with `target_month`, what each month needs from this one
on, and whether the pace is enough. Without `goal_amount`, the category's YNAB target is
used, with its date, when it is a balance to reach.

Here the household has put 200 a month into Holidays since June: from the 800 it holds
today, 2,000 is reached in March 2027, and 171.43 a month would be enough to get there by
then.

<Code code={goal} lang="json" title="get_goal_projection" />

## The bills that do not come every month

Ask *"which big bills are coming this year, and am I ready for them?"*:
`get_upcoming_expenses` lists, month by month, the bills that come less often than every
month. Two sources: YNAB's scheduled transactions every other month to every other year
(or once), and charges found in the last 24 months every 2, 3, 4, 6 or 12 months at about
the same amount (`detected`, with a `confidence`: a heuristic, to check with the user). A
payee with a schedule is counted once, by its schedule.

For each category the bills come out of, it gives what the category holds today, its
target, and `on_track`: whether that, plus what the target is expected to add, covers each
bill by its date. When it does not, `extra_per_month` is what to assign each month beyond
the target to be ready in time. Here the home insurance is already set aside, and the
tennis club's target spreads the 400.00 still missing over the months up to its
membership, due in December.

<Code code={upcomingExpenses} lang="json" title="get_upcoming_expenses" />

## Before the next paycheck

Ask *"what comes out before payday, and how low will checking get?"*:
`get_paycheck_plan` finds the paycheck's rhythm in the history (weekly, every other week,
twice a month or monthly), lists what is due until it (`scheduled`, `recurring` charges on
their usual day, `detected` ones) and walks the balance of the checking and cash accounts
day by day to its lowest point. The paycheck lands at the end of its day, after that day's
bills. Name the payer with `income_payee`, or give `next_pay_date`, `pay_amount` and
`cadence`; `include_next_period` goes on to the paycheck after, and
`include_variable_spending` adds the average everyday spending, otherwise left out. Here
the salary comes on the 28th with nothing due before it, and October's bills leave the
checking account at 3,460.21 at its lowest, on the 24th.

<Code code={paycheckPlan} lang="json" title="get_paycheck_plan (excerpt)" />

## What if?

Ask *"what if we cut restaurants by 20 % and add a 450 car payment from November, or buy
the car outright?"*: `compare_scenarios` makes the same projection as `forecast_balance`, with
the same arguments, as the baseline, then the projection of each scenario (4 at most), a
named list of adjustments:

| Adjustment | What it changes |
|---|---|
| `change_spending` | a category's or group's usual monthly spending, the average of the last 3 full months, by `percent` or to `monthly_spending`, spread over each month's days |
| `add_monthly` | an amount on a `day` of each month from `start` (until `end`): a new charge, or income |
| `add_one_off` | an amount once, on `date` |
| `drop_recurring` | a recurring charge or scheduled payment no longer paid from `start`, found by a word of its `payee` |

Each scenario comes back month by month, with its `difference` from the baseline, the
difference at the end, its lowest balance and that month, the first month below zero and
how each adjustment was applied. Here restaurants cost 112.00 a month, so a fifth less
leaves 22.40 a month; with the car payment from November, the balance is 829.07 lower by
December. Buying the car outright on 15 November takes it below zero that month, to
−1,435.53, even without Streamflix. Four YNAB requests, whatever the number of scenarios.
